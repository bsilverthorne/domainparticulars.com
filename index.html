The problem nobody prices in

In most owner-run businesses, the operating manual doesn't exist — you're the operating manual. How jobs get quoted, which supplier to call when the usual one falls through, what that one customer always needs, why the schedule works the way it does. None of it is written down, because it never had to be.

That works fine until you want to step back, slow down, or sell. A buyer looking at a business that can't run without its owner doesn't just negotiate harder — they discount the price, or they walk. The knowledge in your head is the most valuable thing in the building, and right now it leaves every night in your truck.

How it works

It starts with a conversation, not a contract.

  1. 1
    The Exit Readiness Conversation. About 45 minutes, sitting down with you. Seven plain questions about how the business actually runs — people, process, customers, suppliers, and the things only you know.
  2. 2
    Your Exit Readiness Snapshot. You get one page back: a score of how much of the business currently depends on you, and where the gaps are. No binder, no jargon. One page you can act on.
  3. 3
    The capture work. If the snapshot shows a problem worth fixing, we come alongside and document the business — methodically, area by area — until what's in your head is an asset on paper that a manager can run and a buyer can trust.

What we do

Getting the business out of your head is the core of the work. But not every problem waits for an exit plan — we take on the immediate ones, too. Scoped in writing, delivered on paper.

  • IT & infrastructure assessment. A plain-English read on the systems your business runs on: what's solid, what's fragile, and what it would take to fix.

    What you get: a written assessment with a prioritized list — no sales pitch attached to it.

  • Security assessment. Where the business is exposed — accounts, access, backups, the one password everyone knows — and what to fix first.

    What you get: a prioritized fix list an owner can hand to whoever does the fixing.

  • CMMC Level 2 readiness. For shops that sell into defense supply chains: a gap assessment against the requirements and a realistic path to compliance.

    What you get: a written gap report and a step-by-step readiness plan.

  • Business-systems integration. Making the tools you already pay for actually work together — and documenting the process as it's built, so it isn't trapped in anyone's head.

    What you get: working connections between your systems, plus the written procedure for running them.

Every engagement starts with a fixed scope, agreed in writing before any work begins. Larger work is staged, and each stage ends with a deliverable in your hands and a go/no-go decision that's yours to make. No open-ended hourly meters, no surprises on the invoice.

Why "Domain Particulars"

Every business runs on particulars — the specific rules, relationships, and judgment calls of your domain that make you so hard to replace. They're what a buyer is really buying, and they're almost never written down. Capturing them is the whole job. Hence the name.
